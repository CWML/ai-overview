Prompting and Checking

How to ask well, and how to check what you get back

Five parts of a good prompt

You don’t need all five every time, but the more of them a prompt has, the less you’ll need to correct afterward.

Five parts of an effective prompt
Part What to include Example
Task A clear verb and a specific goal. Break big jobs into steps. “Draft three versions of a recruitment flyer headline…”
Context Background, constraints, audience, what success looks like “…for a study of young adults who use nicotine pouches. IRB-approved wording must stay unchanged…”
Examples A sample of what good (and bad) output looks like “Here’s a headline we liked from a previous study: …”
Format Structure, length, how to mark changes “…under 12 words each, as a numbered list”
Tone Voice and reading level “…friendly, not alarming, 8th-grade reading level.”

Habits that help

  • Let it ask you questions first. End with: “Before you start, ask me any questions you need answered.”
  • Iterate. Treat the first answer as a draft. Say what to change and why.
  • Give it the source. Paste or upload the document (if the data level allows) instead of asking from memory. Answers grounded in a source are more accurate.
  • Ask for its uncertainty. “Which parts of this answer are you least sure about?”
  • Start a new chat for a new topic. Long chats drift, and earlier material can crowd out what matters now.
  • Save prompts that work. In Clarity you can turn a prompt you reuse into a custom agent.
